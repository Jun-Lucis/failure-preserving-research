# Case study: B9 empirical Phase II — retaining unresolved physics

B9 provides a different kind of failure-preserving example from the timing and transfer chains.

The later empirical Phase II did not convert every unresolved quantity into a successful theory. Instead, it retained a mixed evidential state.

## Supported structure

The reduced energy model separates several finite-model scales, including:

- far-separated energetic preference;
- near-scission preference;
- smooth finite-amplitude coexistence;
- local spinodal loss;
- topology-changing pinch-envelope behavior.

The smooth coexistence and spinodal scales were independently repeated in a prospectively frozen held-out-resolution test.

## Negative and unresolved results preserved

The public B9 record explicitly retains:

- a mixed two-case external barrier-magnitude check;
- strong underestimation for one external case;
- two failed prospective near-spinodal exponent tests;
- an unresolved high-resolution nonlocal quartic coefficient;
- no quantitative lambda-to-MeV calibration.

The scientific conclusion is therefore deliberately asymmetric:

```text
metastability architecture: supported in the reduced model
nuclide-specific quantitative closure: not supported
local near-spinodal exponent: unresolved / failed tests retained
```

## Methodological role

This case matters because it shows that failure preservation is not only a route to later PASS.

Sometimes the correct use of failure is to **stop a stronger interpretation**.

The retained failed exponent tests and unresolved coefficient prevent the empirical comparison from being rewritten as a calibrated quantitative nuclear model.

This belongs to the salvage taxonomy as:

- **CLAIM_BOUNDARY_RETENTION**
- **NEGATIVE_CONSTRAINT**
- **EXPERIMENTAL_HANDOFF / EXTERNAL-VALIDATION LIMIT**

## Experimental boundary

The B9 comparison also illustrates the public-data frontier.

Published nuclear data allow a structural comparison, but a minimal reduced model does not reproduce nuclide-specific physics. Moving beyond the present claim would require richer nuclear modeling and, for some questions, domain-specific experimental or evaluated data that are not generated by the individual computational programme itself.
