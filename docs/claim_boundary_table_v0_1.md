# Claim-boundary table for the methodology paper

**Version:** v0.1  
**Date:** 5 October 2026

This table is intended to be used during final manuscript and Zenodo review.

| Topic | Current evidential status | Allowed wording | Avoid / not established |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI and individual research bandwidth | Qualitative longitudinal case evidence + external literature context | AI-assisted reasoning **can increase effective research bandwidth** by helping one researcher formulate, code, debug, inspect, organize, and document more computational work | A quantified productivity multiplier; proof that AI always increases scientific productivity |
| Future value of failure | Supported descriptively by multiple audited BIG chains | Retained negative or limiting results **can remain scientifically active** through reuse, redesign, localization, or claim-boundary enforcement | Every failure is useful; failure is inherently beneficial |
| B19-B40 reuse fraction | Preliminary stage-level audit | In the current broad coding, **21 of 25** negative/limiting source stages have an explicit downstream backward link (**84%**) | 84% is a universal rate; 84% measures causal productivity; 84% applies to all BIG history |
| Change over time | Not yet established | The archive motivates the hypothesis that reuse became more explicit as the programme matured | Failure reuse increased over time as a demonstrated trend |
| Non-retroactivity | Directly documented in several later stages | Parent FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE / INVALID verdicts were retained while successor questions received new identities | Later PASS results rescue or upgrade the original failed test |
| Discovery vs validation | Directly documented in several chains | Old failed data can be used for discovery or redesign, while successor validation uses newly frozen or held-out evaluation where available | Reusing old data and calling the same data independent confirmation |
| Preclaim calibration gates | Directly documented in B34-B35 and other later stages | Readiness/calibration failure can block a claim-bearing experiment until a revised protocol passes predeclared gates | Failed calibration is itself scientific evidence for the final claim |
| Early B3-B18 cases | Archive-verified but nonstandard verdict vocabulary | Early records show analogous patterns of measurement redesign, computational reuse, mechanism redesign, interpretation correction, and transfer-driven enrichment | Treating all early cases as formal prospective FAIL-to-PASS sequences |

| B19E external validation | Public-source supported | The frozen B19 additions did not establish incremental predictive information in the primary external holdout; later budget analysis explained part of the limitation without rescue | External validation confirmed a universal BIG predictor |
| Generality of methodology | Proposal + one longitudinal case study | The architecture is a **testable methodological proposal** motivated by one detailed case | A proven general law of scientific research |
| AI model capability effect | Qualitative case observation only | More capable models may change the practical depth/speed of individual computational work; this is a hypothesis for future study | Attributing specific scientific advances causally to a particular model generation without controlled comparison |

| Experimental handoff | Structural scope boundary | When decisive new measurements do not exist in public data, simulation alone cannot supply them; experimental collaboration becomes necessary | Computational work can substitute for unavailable physical evidence |
| Correctness of BIG | Explicitly outside methodology claim | BIG is a longitudinal case study of research process | The methodology paper validates BIG as a physical theory |

## Core safe formulation

The strongest compact formulation currently supported is:

> **In a documented individual human-AI computational research programme, retained negative and limiting results repeatedly influenced later research actions without being retroactively reclassified; AI-assisted workflow may make this style of high-iteration, failure-preserving research more practical at individual scale.**

The following stronger formulation should remain a hypothesis rather than a conclusion:

> **AI causes failure to become positive research capital in general.**

A safer version is:

> **AI can reduce the marginal cost of exploratory computational branches, while disciplined retention can give some failed branches nonzero future scientific value.**
