# Claim-boundary table for the methodology paper

**Version:** v0.3  
**Date:** 5 October 2026

This table is intended to be used during final manuscript and Zenodo review.

| Topic | Current evidential status | Allowed wording | Avoid / not established |

| AI and individual research bandwidth | Qualitative longitudinal case evidence + external literature context | AI-assisted reasoning **can increase effective research bandwidth** by helping one researcher formulate, code, debug, inspect, organize, and document more computational work | A quantified productivity multiplier; proof that AI always increases scientific productivity |

| Correctness of BIG | Explicitly outside methodology claim | BIG is a longitudinal case study of research process | The methodology paper validates BIG as a physical theory |

## Core safe formulation

The strongest compact formulation currently supported is:

> **In a documented individual human-AI computational research programme, retained negative and limiting results repeatedly influenced later research actions without being retroactively reclassified; AI-assisted workflow may make this style of high-iteration, failure-preserving research more practical at individual scale.**

The following stronger formulation should remain a hypothesis rather than a conclusion:

> **AI causes failure to become positive research capital in general.**

A safer version is:

> **AI can reduce the marginal cost of exploratory computational branches, while disciplined retention can give some failed branches nonzero future scientific value.**

## Temporal-audit note

A 14-transition retained-artifact timestamp audit gives a median source-result-to-successor-freeze interval of about 1.20 h, but no evidence of monotonic shortening across the observed dates (Spearman rho approximately 0.051, p approximately 0.864). The methodology paper should therefore distinguish **reuse prevalence** from **reuse speed**. The later programme is more explicit about lineage; it is not shown to be faster at converting failures into successor protocols.
